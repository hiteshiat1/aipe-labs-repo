# Post 2 — Localhost Angle

If your app only works on localhost, it is still a prototype.

If nobody has confirmed the problem is painful, it is still a guess.

Before you spend 2 weeks building another AI app, answer:

1. Who is this for?
2. What pain does it solve?
3. What do they use today?
4. Will they pay?
5. What is the smallest useful version?

That is why we made the AI Idea Validation Canvas.

# Post 3 — Checklist Post

Before building your next AI product, score the idea:

- Problem severity: /20
- Clear ICP: /15
- Painful alternatives: /15
- Willingness to pay: /15
- Founder advantage: /10
- Speed to MVP: /10
- Distribution access: /10
- Manageable risk: /5

80+ = build MVP  
60–79 = validate first  
40–59 = research more  
Below 40 = pause or reshape

# Post 7 — Practical Prompt Post

A useful prompt before building:

“Act as a skeptical product advisor. Challenge this idea. Identify the target user, painful problem, current alternatives, risky assumptions, willingness-to-pay signals and the smallest useful MVP.”

Then put the answer into a scoring canvas.

That is how you move from excitement to evidence.

# Post 8 — Interview Angle

Before building your AI app, ask 5 target users:

1. How do you solve this today?
2. How often does it happen?
3. What is frustrating?
4. What happens if you do nothing?
5. Have you paid to solve it?
6. What would make you switch?
7. Can I show you a rough version?

Code comes after evidence.
