## Purpose

This document defines the first audience wedge and supporting audience tracks for AI Product Engineering Labs (AIPE Labs). It is the source of truth for how the team talks about the audience across GitHub, Skool, X, Notion templates, playbooks, lead magnets, and future paid products.

AIPE Labs should not try to speak to everyone at the start. The first 60 to 90 days should focus on one sharp audience: people who can already create an AI-generated prototype, but do not know how to turn it into a real product.

---

## Primary Audience Wedge

**AIPE Labs is for AI-assisted builders who can create demos but struggle to productionise, deploy, secure, launch and monetise them.**

This is the first audience wedge for the community, content, templates and build-in-public examples.

### Why this wedge works

- They already believe AI can help them build software.
- They have felt the pain of getting stuck after the prototype.
- They are active on X, GitHub, YouTube, Skool and AI-builder communities.
- They understand the difference between a demo and something real users can use.
- They need guidance across architecture, deployment, GitHub workflow, security, testing, launch and monetisation.
- They can later become buyers of templates, reviews, playbooks, cohorts and implementation support.

---

## Primary Audience Statement

AIPE Labs helps AI-assisted builders move from prototype to production. These builders may use tools like ChatGPT, Claude Code, Cursor, Lovable, Bolt, v0, Replit, Supabase, Firebase, Vercel, Azure, AWS or GCP. They can often generate something quickly, but they need help turning it into a reliable, secure, deployed and launched product.

They are not blocked by starting. They are blocked by finishing, shipping and operating.

---

## Primary Audience Profile

### What they have already done

- Built or generated a demo app.
- Used ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor or another AI coding tool.
- Created frontend screens or a simple prototype.
- Tried to deploy something or connect a database.
- Started a GitHub repo but may not have a clean issue/PR workflow.
- Watched tutorials but still lacks an end-to-end system.

### Where they get stuck

- The app only runs on `localhost:3000`.
- The architecture is unclear.
- Authentication and authorisation are confusing.
- Database design is weak or improvised.
- Cloud deployment is fragile.
- Secrets and environments are not handled properly.
- CI/CD is missing.
- Testing and production checks are missing.
- Observability, logs and error handling are missing.
- They do not know how to launch or get users.
- They do not know what to sell or how to monetise.

### What they want

- A clear path from idea to launched product.
- Practical examples, not theory.
- GitHub issues and implementation plans.
- Architecture decisions they can copy and adapt.
- Deployment playbooks.
- Checklists for production readiness.
- Launch and GTM guidance.
- Confidence that they are building properly.

## Audience Tracks

AIPE Labs can serve multiple audience tracks, but all early public messaging should prioritise the primary wedge.

| Track | Priority | Description | Main Pain | What AIPE Labs Gives Them |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Production AI-assisted builders | Primary | People using AI tools to build apps who need help shipping properly | Demo-to-production gap | Architecture, GitHub workflow, deployment, production readiness and launch systems |
| Non-technical founders | Secondary | Founders who want to validate and manage software builds without being fully dependent on developers | Lack of technical control | Idea validation, PRD, product planning, build oversight and launch checklists |
| Junior engineers and graduates | Secondary | Early-career engineers who want real production experience | Lack of practical delivery experience | GitHub tasks, PR workflow, reviews, deployment practice and portfolio projects |
| Indie hackers | Secondary | Solo builders who want repeatable build-and-launch systems | Fragmented execution | Product workflow, templates, launch playbooks and monetisation systems |
| Consultants and small agencies | Later | Service providers who want reusable delivery assets | Inconsistent delivery | Client playbooks, templates, architecture packs and delivery systems |
| New business owners | Later | Business owners who want to build tools, apps or digital products with AI | Unclear technical path | Simple decision guides, low-risk build plans and launch systems |

## Pain Points by Audience

### 1. Production AI-Assisted Builders

- They can create demos but cannot finish products.
- They are unsure how to structure frontend, backend and database.
- They do not know when AI-generated code is safe enough.
- They lack production checklists.
- They are unsure how to deploy, test and monitor.
- They need launch and revenue guidance.

### 2. Non-Technical Founders

- They do not know how to convert an idea into a buildable scope.
- They struggle to manage developers or AI-generated output.
- They cannot judge whether architecture decisions are sensible.
- They need a way to validate ideas before spending money.
- They want to launch without wasting months.

### 3. Junior Engineers and Graduates

- They lack real-world product delivery experience.
- They know syntax but not production systems.
- They need portfolio-quality projects.
- They need experience with GitHub issues, PRs, CI/CD and cloud.
- They need mentorship and review discipline.

### 4. Indie Hackers

- They start too many ideas and finish too few.
- They lack repeatable systems.
- They need market validation before building.
- They need faster launch cycles.
- They want templates and operating systems they can reuse.

### 5. Consultants and Small Agencies

- They need reusable delivery frameworks.
- They want to productise services.
- They need client-ready templates and checklists.
- They want faster discovery, architecture and launch workflows.

### 6. New Business Owners

- They want software but do not know what to build first.
- They need simple, low-risk entry points.
- They need to understand costs, scope and launch steps.
- They want useful tools, not technical complexity.

## Premium Intent Signals

A member is showing premium intent when they ask for:

- A complete template instead of a free checklist.
- Help reviewing their product idea.
- Architecture feedback.
- GitHub issue breakdown for their own product.
- Deployment help.
- Security/authentication review.
- Database design support.
- Launch plan support.
- Pricing or monetisation guidance.
- Accountability or cohort support.

These signals should be collected weekly by Ruthwik from Skool and by Sujay from X replies and DMs.

## Proof They Need Before Trusting AIPE Labs

The audience will trust AIPE Labs when they see proof, not claims.

### Proof assets required

- Real GitHub issues and PRs
- Public build progress
- Architecture diagrams
- Working demos
- Deployment walkthroughs
- Production readiness checklists
- Screenshots of live apps
- Launch metrics
- Honest mistakes and fixes
- Before/after examples
- Skool member wins
- Templates being used by real people

## Messaging Rules

### Use these phrases

- Move from demo to production
- Build and launch real software
- Production-grade AI-assisted software
- AI product engineering
- GitHub-driven delivery
- Architecture, cloud and launch systems
- Less toy demos, more shipped products
- If it only runs on localhost, it is still a prototype

### Avoid overusing these phrases

- Vibe coding
- Prompt hacks
- Get rich quick
- Passive income
- One-click startup
- AI magic
- No-code only
- Build an app in 10 minutes

The phrase “vibe coding” can be used occasionally for reach, but the serious category is **AI product engineering**.

## Skool Content Mapping

Skool is the depth and accountability channel. The audience strategy should appear in Skool as a member-facing post, not as an internal strategy document.

### Recommended Skool post title

**Who AIPE Labs Is For**

### Skool post angle

AIPE Labs is for builders who are tired of stopping at demos.

You are in the right place if:

- You can generate an app but do not know how to productionise it.
- Your project works on localhost but is not ready for real users.
- You are confused about architecture, auth, database, cloud deployment or CI/CD.
- You want to launch, not just experiment.
- You want templates, checklists and real build examples.

### Skool introduction prompts

Ask members:

1. What are you building?
2. Where are you stuck?
3. Is your product still a prototype, or is it already deployed?
4. What do you want to ship in the next 30 days?

## X Content Mapping

X is the discovery channel. Content should use sharp pain points and clear hooks.

### X content angles

1. Localhost is not production.
2. AI demos are easy; production is the hard part.
3. The new builder problem is finishing, not starting.
4. GitHub issues turn ideas into execution.
5. Architecture beats prompt chaos.
6. Deployment, auth, database and launch are where builders get stuck.
7. AIPE Labs is where demos become products.

### Example X post 1

Most AI apps do not fail because the idea is bad.

They fail because the builder stops at:

- localhost:3000
- no auth
- no database plan
- no error handling
- no CI/CD
- no launch plan
- no customer feedback

AIPE Labs is for builders who want to move beyond demos and ship real products.

### Example X post 2

If you can build a demo with Claude or Cursor but get stuck at deployment, architecture, auth, database, GitHub issues, testing or launch...

You are exactly who we are building AIPE Labs for.

The new builder problem is not “Can I generate code?”

It is:

- Can I structure it?
- Can I review it?
- Can I secure it?
- Can I deploy it?
- Can I launch it?
- Can I turn it into a product?

That is the gap AIPE Labs is focused on.

Vibe coding gets you a prototype.

Product engineering gets you:

- architecture
- GitHub workflow
- auth
- database
- deployment
- CI/CD
- observability
- launch
- revenue

AIPE Labs is where we build the second part.
